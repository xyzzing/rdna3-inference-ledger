## min-p is a chain stop rule — and a donor threshold turned out to key on nothing

The drafter's `--spec-min-p` (0.70 in production) was pinned three ways — live argv, the parse
site, and all three truncation loci found at the predicted source lines, outside any dirty
tree region. Its semantics, from the governing loop condition (`>= threshold`): **a run-length
stop rule on the drafter's own confidence** — the offered chain stops at the first draft token
under min-p, so *lowering* min-p can only widen the offered width, never shrink it. Any future
A/B on this knob is a width experiment, not a quality experiment.

Six fixture cells were frozen with per-cell occupancy, true W/T, output budgets, cache
lifecycle and request-ID join keys — including the definitional note that the deep p-reg cell
is **69,861 tokens**: "94K" was never one number, and per-cell values govern. A holdout set
was reserved **before** threshold selection, leakage-checked (needle-absence + containment,
fail-hard), with one disclosed weakness (a short-holdout prompt at jaccard 0.707 vs its
development twin — excluded from independence claims).

Packet C (the "512-token boundary" experiment inherited from a donor proposal) resolved as a
trap-fact: the pinned engine's governing boundary is **64 fresh tokens** (`short_read` default
64, the verify-window selector) — appends ≤64 fresh tokens go through verify windows, 65–511
go through batched prefill, and **511 vs 512 crosses nothing**. The donor's 512 threshold is
not a constant in this engine. Pin the actual constant from the pinned source before building
a battery around a number you read somewhere else. (The battery itself: NOT_RUN — deferred,
secondary, not a decode candidate.)

## The IQ3_S codebook is 16 signed levels wide, not ~125

The Nov 13 close listed "an IQ3_S integer-dot replay if priced" in the adopted
next-experiment order. The round plan's feasibility premise expected **±grid ≈ 125 signed
levels** — above the 16-symbol budget — i.e. it expected the idea to die at enumeration. It
was enumerated anyway (stdlib-only tool over the pinned decoder source, `--selfcheck`
reproducible: 276 extremal synthetic blocks through a hand-ported decoder, agree=true), and
the premise is wrong:

- The pinned decoder's **512-entry `iq3s_grid`** (ggml vendor tree, `ggml-common.h`) packs 4
  nibbles per entry, and **every nibble that occurs is an odd magnitude in {1,3,5,7,9,11,13,15}**
  — all 2048 nibbles verified, multiplicities 436/344/327/271/223/185/112/150. One independent
  sign bit per nibble. So the distinct signed unscaled levels are **exactly 16**: {±1, ±3, …,
  ±15}. The ~125 premise conflated grid *entries* (or the 174-value folded-scale set) with
  per-weight stored magnitudes. A second, independent re-parse of the table reproduced the
  multiplicities digit-for-digit before the verdict was accepted.
- **The obvious mapping is the trap**: plain signed-INT4 identity fails (±9…±15 do not fit
  [−8,7] — confirmed by exhaustive test). The unique exact affine map (exhaustive search over
  A ∈ [1,8], B ∈ [−31,31] plus every pattern class) is `sym(v) = (v+15)/2`, i.e.
  `int(s) = 2s − 15`; the dot becomes `2·Σ s·u − 15·Σ u` — an ordinary integer nibble dot plus
  one row-sum side term per subgroup. Block scales are preserved verbatim.
- **Byte ledger**: 134 vs 110 B per 256 weights (**+21.8%**) before alignment; and when both
  representations live (sidecar + original), the honest retention budget is **244 B per 256
  weights** — 29.81 MB per layer-17 gate/up expert set — not the +24 B difference alone.

So the **format side is proven**: a lossless ≤16-symbol integer-dot representation of IQ3_S
exists for this decoder, and anyone running IQ3_S on integer paths can store the nibble
directly instead of reconstructing grid + signs per weight. What is *not* known is whether it
pays: the exposure product `f × h × (1 − 1/s)` needs `f` (the exposed gate/up share of decode
wall — note the head-gemv 4.9–5.9% figure already in the ledger is the *draft head*, not
gate/up) and `h` (time-weighted residency coverage of routed experts), and no saved record
prices either. The bar is ≥0.0196 of decode wall (the ≥1.020 gate); with `f` and `h`
unpriced, the candidate is neither provable nor closable — **BLOCKED on named counters**, not
closed. Device-level exactness: NOT_RUN (host-model proof only; a real-block cross-check is
blocked on installed GGUF readers not knowing this artifact's quant type 42 — named for the
next reader).

The worker first recorded this CLOSED; review corrected it to **BLOCKED**, and the distinction
is the point: the candidate is real and transferable — what's missing is its pricing input
(the per-window `(n_groups, n_ent, widest group)` triplet exists in no saved record). Under
the predeclared ruling table, "missing inputs" maps to BLOCKED, which keeps the candidate
eligible to compete when the counters exist; CLOSED would have foreclosed it on an absence of
bookkeeping rather than a measured negative.
